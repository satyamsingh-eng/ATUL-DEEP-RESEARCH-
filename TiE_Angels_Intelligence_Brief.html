The Local-to-Global Funnel

Startups do not pitch "TiE Global" directly. You apply to your closest or most strategically aligned chapter (e.g., TiE Pittsburgh). The local chapter conducts due diligence. If the local chapter commits capital (the "anchor check"), the deal is immediately syndicated to the global platform.

The mechanism: Win Atul Bansal in Pittsburgh, and he broadcasts the investment opportunity across the global TiE network, tapping capital from Silicon Valley, Seattle, Boston, and India.

The Capital Structure (SPVs)

Instead of 30 individual angels on your cap table, TiE chapters pool capital into Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs). A single entity (e.g., "TiE Pittsburgh Angels LLC") appears on your cap table.

Why it matters: It keeps the cap table completely clean for future Series A institutional VCs, while giving you the combined network access of every individual LP in that SPV.
