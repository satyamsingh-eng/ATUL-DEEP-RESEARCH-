## 05 — Every Company He Founded or Led

### Laurel Networks (1999–2005)

Founded in Pittsburgh in 1999 as a provider of IP/MPLS multiservice edge routers, targeting telecom carriers with its flagship ST200 Service Edge Router. Founders: Atul Bansal (President/CEO/Chairman) plus five other FORE Systems veterans in their 30s at founding, combining deep expertise across software, hardware, switching, and routing. Funding came in at least four/five rounds: Round 1 ($12.3M, led by New Enterprise Associates and Rein Capital); Round 2 ($60M, led by NEA, Trinity Ventures, Worldview Technology Partners, and WorldCom Venture Fund); a $20M third round in September 2003 with new investor CIENA (which also became a reseller partner) and continued participation from NEA, Trinity Ventures, and Worldview Technology Partners. Total capital raised reached $97.3–120M depending on source. The company employed roughly 130–150 people at peak. Outcome: acquired by ECI Telecom for $88 million cash in a deal announced May 16, 2005 and closed by June 6, 2005, becoming ECI's Data Networking Division.[24][25][31][26][27][32][33]

### Timesys Corporation (2007–2023)

Timesys, an existing company founded in 1995/1996 in Pittsburgh, was a pioneer in open-source embedded Linux and later cybersecurity/SBOM tooling (its "Vigiles" product line). Bansal joined as CEO in April 2007, explicitly to expand the company's non-proprietary, open-source Linux commitment. Over his 16-year tenure, Timesys closed at least ten distinct funding/deal events (accelerator funding, multiple later-stage VC rounds in 2003, 2005, 2006, 2007, 2008, 2009, a private secondary transaction, and finally the December 2023 acquisition), raising a cumulative $40.5–44.8 million from a syndicate of 13 investors including Adams Capital Management, The Hillman Company, Philips Electronics, Nissin Electric, BET Associates, BRU Holdings, Elderstreet Investments, and Future-Fund. A June 2005 $6 million round was explicitly led by Adams Capital Management with Hillman and Philips participating. His executive team included Akshay Bhat (CTO), Al Feczko (VP Sales & Field Engineering), Maciej Halasz (VP Technology), and — notably — Dr. Rakesh Thapar as CTO and Managing Director of Timesys Technologies India, LLP, the company's India subsidiary that Bansal continued to chair after the Lynx acquisition. The company served 1,000+ customers across medical, automotive, industrial, networking, aerospace, and IoT sectors. Outcome: acquired by Lynx Software Technologies (an OceanSound Partners portfolio company) on December 12, 2023.[29][34][28][35][36][37][38][39][2]

## 06 — Founder Story

**Laurel Networks** was created to exploit a specific technical opportunity: applying the founding team's FORE Systems-honed expertise in high-performance switching and routing to a new category — IP/MPLS multiservice edge routers for telecom carriers — at a moment when carriers needed to converge voice, data, and video onto IP infrastructure. The differentiation was credibility: NEA partner Rob Coneybeer explicitly cited the team's prior billion-dollar product experience at FORE as the reason "the carriers know these guys". The hardest period was almost certainly the two-plus years of pre-revenue fundraising and product development (1999–2001), during which Bansal was commuting weekly between Washington, D.C. and Pittsburgh to raise capital and build the business. Strategic decisions that shaped the outcome included bringing in CIENA as a strategic investor and reseller in 2003 — broadening carrier-level distribution — which likely improved the company's positioning ahead of its 2005 sale.[31][26][27][33][23]

**Timesys** was an existing company (founded 1995/1996) that Bansal joined as an outside CEO in 2007, rather than founding — his personal contribution was steering a mid-stage embedded-software company toward a sustained bet on open-source Linux as opposed to proprietary embedded operating systems, and later pivoting the company's product emphasis toward cybersecurity/SBOM compliance (Vigiles) as regulatory pressure (FDA, EU Cyber Resilience Act, White House Executive Order 14028) created new demand. His personally driven strategic decisions, evidenced by his own quotes in company press releases, centered on expanding Vigiles' SBOM capabilities to meet emerging medical-device and critical-infrastructure compliance mandates.[28][36]
