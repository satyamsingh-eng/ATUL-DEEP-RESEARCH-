03 / RECONSTRUCTED TIMELINE

The arc, with transition logic.

Each step explains the operating system he moved into—not merely the role title. Where causality is not documented, it is not invented.

1984–1989

Education: engineering-to-computer-science foundation.

A source-dated Timesys biography reports B.S. Chemical Engineering from IIT Kanpur (1984), followed by M.S. Chemical Engineering (1987) and M.S. Computer Science (1989) from the University of Rhode Island.

Transition logic: the professionally relevant signal is the cross-disciplinary shift from process engineering into computer science before the infrastructure career. The education record is historical biography, not a current affiliation.
 
1988–1995

Router engineering and technical management at Digital Equipment Corporation.

Company biography places Atul in engineering and technical-management positions for router products at Digital Equipment Corporation.

Transition logic: establishes protocol/networking depth that later becomes the backbone of his operator identity; independent contemporary corroboration was not located in this cycle.
 
1995–1999

FORE Systems: IP-routing and multilayer-switching leadership.

Atul joined FORE in 1995 and the source-dated biography identifies senior IP-routing and multilayer-switching roles. The material finding is the repeat cluster: Laurel’s co-founders and technical leadership also show FORE lineage.

Transition logic: FORE supplied the technical, product, and commercial bench for the next company—not just Atul’s resume line.
   
Oct 1999–2005

Laurel Networks: founder-led carrier-edge company.

Official snapshots show early CEO/President leadership. A 1 February 2004 contemporaneous report then records Don Pyle’s CEO succession and Atul’s President/COO role before the 2005 sale. Board sources identify venture governance and capital context; the final pre-sale newsletter shows carrier automation, security/provisioning, a Grande FTTH deployment, and a Telindus UK reseller route.

Transition logic: Laurel combined carrier IP/MPLS product depth with international channels and an investor board—closer to an enterprise platform system than a point product. The 2004 CEO handoff is documented; its internal rationale is not.
      
Jun 2005–2007

ECI acquisition, post-sale operating role, and integration.

ECI acquired Laurel for $88m cash and formed the Data Networking Division. A source-dated Timesys biography says Atul became that division’s COO. ECI later disclosed an early division operating loss, then folded the technology into Transport Networking.

Transition logic: the evidence supports a founder-to-post-sale operating transition. It also shows that a strategic buyer can retain technology while facing integration/economic pressure; that is not an attribution of success or failure to Atul.
    
Apr 2007–Dec 2023

Timesys CEO: board-led recruitment into embedded/open-source infrastructure.

Appointment followed a board process involving Adams Capital context. The company narrative describes global distribution and semiconductor alliances.

Transition logic: a move from carrier network equipment to recurring lifecycle/security tooling—maintaining the systems-layer, technical-partnership operating pattern.
  
Dec 2023–Nov 2024 source-dated

Timesys → Lynx sale; post-sale open-source business development signal.

Lynx acquired Timesys for mission-critical edge software; official material later quotes Atul in an open-source business-development role tied to SBOM/vulnerability lifecycle work.

Transition logic: post-sale integration appears to place his expertise at the product/go-to-market boundary of open-source security. That is the last primary-source operating signal—not proof of 2026 status.
