# Atul Bansal — Professional Kundali
## Board decision brief

> **PRIVATE · C3A Labs · SARVAX**
> **Purpose:** decide what C3A can responsibly ask of Atul Bansal now—and what it must not assume.
> **Research boundary:** public, regulatory, archived-company, counterparty, academic, and privacy-minimised vendor evidence reviewed through **13 August 2026**.
> **Identity guardrail:** this brief covers only the executive associated with **Digital Equipment Corporation → FORE Systems → Laurel Networks → ECI → Timesys → Lynx**. Same-name narratives involving LivLyt, ZenAdmin, Careem, McKinsey, KPMG, or unrelated academic/board records are excluded.

## The decision

**Run one 25-minute operator-review conversation. Do not approach Atul as a current investor, advisor, board member, customer connector, or source of introductions.**

The evidence supports a substantial enterprise-infrastructure operating record, a documented founder transition, a later company acquisition during his CEO tenure, and dated angel-platform governance.[1][7][8][11][12][16]

It does **not** establish his 2026 employer, current title, active investment mandate, cheque authority, advisory or board capacity, wealth-management access, relationship strength, or willingness to help C3A.

### The board posture

**GO — ask for a disciplined operating critique of one real SARVAX workflow.**
This is proportionate to the verified record: enterprise infrastructure, product/company formation, acquisition transition, and operating leadership.[1][2][8][11][12]

**NO-GO — do not ask for money, introductions, a title, a customer route, or permission to use his name.**
Historic company transactions, TiE governance, investor lists, institutional overlaps, and stale company pages do not establish present authority or access.[3][7][16][17][18]

**RESET — ask what he is focused on only after delivering value.**
The correct relationship frame—operating, advising, investing, or something else—is unknown and must come from him, not historical association.

> **Founder recommendation — INFERENCE:** Earn one useful conversation around a specific enterprise-execution problem. If Atul identifies a relevant lane himself, C3A can make a narrower follow-up ask with fresh permission. Anything more ambitious would overstate the record.

## The evidence model

### FACT
A statement directly supported by a cited regulatory, official, archived-company, counterparty, academic, or contemporaneous industry source. Use it exactly as worded and dated.

### INFERENCE
A constrained synthesis of facts. It can guide the first conversation, but it cannot become access, motive, authority, or a present-tense role.

### WITHHELD
A material claim the reviewed record does not establish. State the gap plainly. Do not fill it with vendor data, stale listings, roster overlap, or company mythology.

## What the record actually establishes

### 1. Systems-layer operating depth before company formation

A source-dated Timesys biography places Atul in router-product engineering and technical-management roles at Digital Equipment Corporation from **1988–1995**, then in senior IP-routing and multilayer-Ethernet-switching roles at FORE Systems from **1995**.[1]

The same biography reports an IIT Kanpur / University of Rhode Island engineering-to-computer-science education path. **Boundary:** these are historical company-biography claims, not independent identity-matched university or employer verification.[1]

### 2. A real carrier-infrastructure company, not a résumé line

Laurel Networks was founded in **October 1999**. Archived Laurel materials identify Atul as co-founder and, at different source dates, President and CEO. They also establish formal company/board context including NEA and Trinity representation—not personal relationships or current investor access.[2][3]

Laurel’s own investor page reported nearly **$90 million** of company funding; ECI’s later disclosure reported **$118 million**. These are dated company disclosures with different timing/scope—not a contradiction and not personal economics.[4][9]

An archived technical-leadership page also indicates a broader former-FORE/carrier-networking leadership cluster inside Laurel. It supports historic company lineage, not a current relationship or access map.[5]

Before its sale, Laurel documented carrier automation/security features, a Grande Communications FTTH deployment, and a Telindus UK reseller mechanism. ECI’s acquisition materials described roughly **150 employees**, **$18 million** in trailing-twelve-month sales, sales/support across the United States, Europe, and Asia, and named customers including Level 3, Korea Telecom/KT, Dacom, and Arsys.[6][8][9]

**What matters for SARVAX — INFERENCE:** differentiated enterprise infrastructure became credible through a combination of product, channels, named deployment, and institutional governance. This is more decision-useful than the shallow claim that Atul “sold a company.”

### 3. The Laurel handoff and exit must be described precisely

A contemporaneous report dated **1 February 2004** says Don Pyle succeeded co-founder/CEO Atul Bansal as Laurel CEO and Atul became President and COO.[7]

ECI acquired **100% of Laurel’s outstanding common shares for $88 million cash** in June 2005.[8][9]

**Required language:** Atul was **not Laurel CEO at the 2005 ECI sale**. He was President/COO after the documented handoff. The deal does not establish his personal proceeds, negotiating role, wealth, or present investor activity.[7][8][9]

### 4. A second enterprise-software leadership arc

Timesys appointed Atul CEO on **10 April 2007** after a board-led search.[11]

Lynx acquired Timesys in **December 2023**. The transaction materials describe the combination of Lynx mission-critical edge software with Timesys Linux development tools, cybersecurity solutions, and engineering services.[12][13][14]

The latest retained primary operating signal is a **November 2024** body-dated Lynx statement identifying Atul as Vice President, Open-Source Business Development in the context of embedded vulnerability management and SBOM lifecycle work.[15]

**Present-tense boundary:** this is a historical role signal, not proof of a 2026 Lynx title, employment relationship, transition status, or availability.[15]

### 5. Dated ecosystem governance, not current capital authority

A February 2021 SEC Form D for TiE Pittsburgh Angels identifies Atul as Co-Managing Director and an executive officer/director. It also identifies Ganesh Mani and Raymond Milhem in formal governance roles.[16]

TiE Pittsburgh and TiE Angels publish organizational context that includes Atul, but those pages do not establish active investment behavior, individual deal participation, portfolio, allocation authority, cheque size, a current mandate, or a fundraising route for C3A.[17][18]

Carnegie Mellon’s public profile establishes Ganesh Mani’s financial-services AI work. This is thematic adjacency only: it does **not** prove a personal relationship, a warm route, availability, or permission to introduce.[19]

CEO ally is the named sell-side advisor in the Timesys–Lynx transaction. A prior source-dated Innosential page capture carried a historical Timesys descriptor; current public root/About pages do not name Atul. None of this establishes current personal access, an Innosential operating role, or a board/advisory role.[12][20][21][22]

## What C3A should test—without pretending it is already true

**INFERENCE:** Atul’s verified history is closest to enterprise infrastructure under transition: router and carrier-edge work; company formation; a strategic acquisition; embedded Linux and lifecycle/security work; and post-acquisition operating context.[1][2][10][12][15]

That makes one narrow question worth testing:

> Can Atul pressure-test whether SARVAX is becoming an enterprise-grade, approval-aware execution business—not merely an AI feature narrative?

This does **not** establish direct wealth-management expertise, regulated-financial-services buyer access, AI investment expertise, or a willingness to advise C3A.

### The right artifact for the first conversation

Show **one** SARVAX workflow with:

1. a real operational trigger;
2. a structured action packet rather than a generic summary;
3. a named human approval boundary for risk-sensitive action;
4. traceable, controlled completion; and
5. one measurable proof hypothesis.

Ask for criticism of the **problem, proof threshold, control boundary, business model, and focus**. Do not ask whether he likes “AI agents.”

## Recommended relationship motion

### 1. Earn the conversation
Request a 25-minute operator critique using a one-page SARVAX brief.

**Proof goal:** determine whether the problem and trust boundary are legible to an experienced enterprise operator.
**Do not do:** fundraising pitch, advisor offer, customer ask, title claim.

### 2. Demonstrate listening
Return a short note: what changed, what remains open, and what evidence C3A will produce next.

**Proof goal:** demonstrate execution discipline rather than extract access.
**Do not do:** send a broad follow-up deck or repeat the first ask.

### 3. Reset the present context
Ask directly: **“What are you focused on now—operating, advising, investing, or something else?”**

**Proof goal:** resolve the highest-impact unknown without guessing from old pages.
**Do not do:** treat historic titles or platform listings as an answer.

### 4. Let him define the next permission
If he signals interest, ask whether **one category** of operator or founder feedback would be useful.

**Proof goal:** let relevance and permission come from him.
**Do not do:** name people, request a contact list, or assert relationships.

### 5. Consider formal engagement only after pull
Discuss an advisory or capital role only after repeat value exchange and an explicit invitation.

**Proof goal:** separate genuine fit from polite engagement.
**Do not do:** make premature equity, cheque, or introduction requests.

### Questions worth asking

1. Which part of this workflow needs stronger proof before an enterprise buyer will trust it?
2. Where would you cut scope to make the first wedge commercially legible?
3. What would make the control boundary real rather than marketing language?
4. Which proof signal matters more at this stage: accountable owner, measurable pain, repeatability, or integration readiness?
5. What is the most useful way for you to engage today—if at all?

### Questions that are premature

- Will you invest?
- Can you introduce investors, wealth-management buyers, or strategic acquirers?
- Will you become an advisor?
- Can we use your name?
- Can you connect us to Ganesh, TiE, Lynx, CEO ally, or former Laurel/Timesys investors?
